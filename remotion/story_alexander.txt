Two thousand three hundred years ago, a young king named Alexander marched out of Macedon with a small army.
He crossed the sea and crushed the mighty Persian army, then rode into Egypt, where the priests crowned him a god.
He pushed east through deserts and mountains, building cities wherever he went.
His soldiers carried heavy swords and shields for thousands of miles, but at the river Indus they were exhausted, and they refused to go on.
Alexander turned back, and in Babylon he fell ill and died, just before his thirty third birthday.
He left behind an empire stretching across three continents, and a legend that still echoes today.
