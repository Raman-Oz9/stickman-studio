Before sunrise, a poor worker walked to the factory with an empty stomach.
For ten hours he built machines, and his wage barely paid the rent.
Meanwhile, a rich businessman sat in a glass office, counting the profits of his company.
The rich man did not work harder. He owned the machines, and the machines made money while he slept.
"Money makes money," the businessman said with a smile.
The worker saved a little each month, but prices rose faster than his pay.
The businessman borrowed cheap from the bank and bought more buildings, and the rent from the poor paid his debts.
When a crisis came, the businessman was rescued, and the worker lost his job.
Rich families taught their children how money works, while the poor were taught only to work for it.
So the gap grew wider every year, until the worker finally learned the rule: own assets, not just a salary.
